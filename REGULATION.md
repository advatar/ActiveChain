As of **July 2026**, both the EU and the US have dedicated stablecoin legislation, but they approach the subject differently:

- The **EU’s MiCA regime is already operational**.
- The **US GENIUS Act has been enacted**, but its main requirements are still being implemented and are expected to become effective no later than **18 January 2027**, unless final implementing regulations trigger an earlier date. ## European Union: MiCA

The principal legislation is the **Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation**, Regulation (EU) 2023/1114, commonly called **MiCA**. Its stablecoin provisions have applied since **30 June 2024**, while the broader MiCA framework has applied since 30 December 2024.  [oai_citation:1‡EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1114/oj/eng?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

MiCA does not use “stablecoin” as its principal legal category. It divides them into:

### 1. E-money tokens, or EMTs

An EMT seeks to maintain its value by referencing **one official currency**, such as:

- EURC referencing the euro
- USDC or USDT referencing the US dollar

An EMT issuer must generally be authorised in the EU as either:

- a **credit institution**, meaning a bank; or
- an **electronic-money institution**.

The token is legally treated as electronic money. Holders must have a direct claim against the issuer and a right to redeem the token **at par value**, without the issuer imposing an ordinary redemption fee. Issuers and crypto service providers may not pay interest or equivalent time-related benefits on EMT holdings.  [oai_citation:2‡EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A32023R1114&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### 2. Asset-referenced tokens, or ARTs

An ART references something other than a single official currency, such as:

- several currencies
- commodities
- crypto-assets
- a basket combining different assets

ART issuers generally require specific MiCA authorisation. They must maintain a segregated reserve of assets, observe governance and prudential requirements, publish an approved crypto-asset white paper, and provide redemption rights under MiCA.  [oai_citation:3‡EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A02023R1114-20240109&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Significant stablecoins

The European Banking Authority can classify an ART or EMT as **significant**, based on matters such as its size, number of holders, transaction activity and cross-border importance. Significant tokens face more intensive EBA supervision and additional capital, liquidity and risk-management requirements.  [oai_citation:4‡EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/IT-EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32023R1114&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Practical consequences

A stablecoin cannot simply be issued abroad and freely marketed throughout the EU. Its issuer and offering structure must satisfy MiCA. EU crypto-asset service providers were instructed to stop facilitating the acquisition of non-MiCA-compliant stablecoins, with national authorities expected to secure compliance by the end of the first quarter of 2025.  [oai_citation:5‡esma.europa.eu](https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-and-european-commission-publish-guidance-non-mica-compliant-arts-and-emts?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

MiCA also overlays:

- AML and counter-terrorist-financing obligations
- the EU “travel rule” for crypto transfers
- custody and safeguarding requirements
- market-abuse rules
- operational-resilience requirements under DORA
- licensing requirements for exchanges and other crypto-asset service providers

An important feature is that MiCA treats many ordinary fiat-backed stablecoins as a regulated form of **electronic money**, not merely as generic crypto-assets.

## United States: GENIUS Act

The US enacted the **Guiding and Establishing National Innovation for U.S. Stablecoins Act**, or **GENIUS Act**, on **18 July 2025**. It is Public Law 119-27 and creates the first comprehensive federal framework specifically for **payment stablecoins**.  [oai_citation:6‡The White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/2025/07/the-president-signed-into-law-s-1582/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The law generally restricts US payment-stablecoin issuance to a **permitted payment stablecoin issuer**. Depending on the issuer, supervision may be federal or state-based. Eligible structures include certain:

- bank subsidiaries
- federally qualified non-bank issuers
- state-qualified issuers

Digital-asset service providers will generally be prohibited from offering a payment stablecoin in the US unless its issuer is permitted under the Act or qualifies under the foreign-issuer provisions.  [oai_citation:7‡OCC.gov](https://www.occ.treas.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-3.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Reserve requirements

Payment stablecoins must be backed at least **one-for-one** by permitted reserve assets. These principally include highly liquid, low-risk assets such as:

- US dollars and Federal Reserve notes
- qualifying bank deposits
- short-term US Treasury obligations
- qualifying Treasury-backed repurchase agreements
- certain government money-market funds

The framework is therefore intended for fully reserved payment stablecoins rather than fractional-reserve private money.  [oai_citation:8‡The White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/research/2026/04/effects-of-stablecoin-yield-prohibition-on-bank-lending/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Issuers must also meet requirements concerning:

- public reserve disclosures
- regular examination or attestation
- redemption policies
- capital, liquidity and risk management
- custody and segregation of reserves
- Bank Secrecy Act and sanctions compliance
- customer identification
- insolvency treatment and priority for stablecoin holders

Federal agencies were still proposing detailed implementing regulations during 2026, including weekly and quarterly reporting, prudential requirements and customer-identification programs.  [oai_citation:9‡OCC.gov](https://www.occ.treas.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-24.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### No issuer-paid yield

The GENIUS Act prohibits the issuer from directly paying interest or yield merely for holding the payment stablecoin. The exact treatment of rewards paid by exchanges or unrelated intermediaries is more nuanced and depends on the final rules and the structure of the arrangement.  [oai_citation:10‡Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/payment-stablecoins-and-cross-border-payments-benefits-and-implications-for-monetary-policy-20260330.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Not a bank deposit

A US payment stablecoin is expressly **not an FDIC-insured deposit**. Issuers may not represent that it benefits from federal deposit insurance.  [oai_citation:11‡OCC.gov](https://www.occ.treas.gov/topics/charters-and-licensing/interpretations-and-decisions/2026/cd1380.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Foreign issuers

A foreign stablecoin issuer may gain US market access, but it must satisfy statutory conditions, including being subject to a sufficiently comparable foreign regulatory regime and registering or otherwise qualifying with the relevant US authority. The system is therefore not intended to permit completely unregulated offshore issuers to serve US customers.

## Main differences

| Issue | European Union | United States |
|---|---|---|
| Principal law | MiCA | GENIUS Act |
| Current status | Fully applicable | Enacted; implementing rules still being completed |
| Main category | EMTs and ARTs | Payment stablecoins |
| Single-currency stablecoin | Legally treated as e-money | Separate payment-stablecoin category |
| Eligible issuers | For EMTs, banks or authorised e-money institutions | Permitted bank, federal non-bank or state-qualified issuers |
| Reserves | Detailed safeguarding and reserve rules depending on token type | At least 1:1 in specified liquid assets |
| Redemption | Par redemption required for EMTs | Timely redemption under disclosed policies and regulatory standards |
| Holder interest | Prohibited for EMTs and ARTs | Direct issuer-paid interest prohibited |
| Deposit insurance | Normally not a bank deposit merely because it is a token | Expressly not FDIC insured |
| Regulatory style | One harmonised EU framework with passporting | Federal floor with federal and qualifying state supervision |
| Effective position in July 2026 | Operating regime | Transitional rulemaking phase |

## Commercial interpretation

For a company issuing a euro or dollar stablecoin:

- **In the EU**, you normally need an EU bank or electronic-money-institution structure for a single-currency token, plus MiCA compliance. Once properly authorised, the EU structure can provide access across the single market.
- **In the US**, you will need permitted-issuer status under the GENIUS Act once it becomes effective, with 1:1 qualifying reserves and either federal or qualifying state supervision.
- A single global token may need **parallel legal issuance structures**, for example an EU EMT issuer and a US permitted payment-stablecoin issuer, even when both tokens use the same brand and blockchain infrastructure.

The EU framework is more tightly integrated into existing electronic-money law. The US framework is more explicitly designed as a new category of privately issued, fully reserved digital payment instrument.
